## 2. What exists now

### 2.1 Pinned / busted / backup / buy-back

Rules (the spec the brother wrote down):

- An officer that **touches** a free player **pins** them (held down on the ground). Touch = within `GuardAI.catchRadius` (1.6 m) while chasing.
- A pinned player has **12 s** until they are **busted** (arrested).
- If the pinning officer is **hit by another player** (melee or a thrown item), the pinned player is freed.
- When **nobody is free** (everyone pinned or busted) the whole team is busted: run over (`HeistPhase.Failed`). With one player, a pin is an instant bust, same as before.
- After a guard calls backup, **another officer is called every 30 s** until the floor ends.
- Busted players can be **bought back from the vendor** once the team is at the Loading Docks. The vendor says: *"One of your friends got got. You're lucky I have someone on the inside"*.

Design decisions worth knowing (change them if the team disagrees):

- **Busted bodies stay in the world**, lying down, and ride along in the elevator car (the floor loader puts every player in the car). They are invisible to guards and ignored by the "everyone aboard" check.
- A busted player's **backpack is left alone** (not dropped). Carried items in the hands are dropped.
- Buy-back price is `300 + 50 x (level they were busted on)`: $350 on floor 1, $900 on floor 12. Tune in `CustodyRules.BuyBackPrice`. Only available at the docks (that is the only floor with a vendor).
- Only a **chasing** officer pins (same rule the old instant-bust used). Walking into an idle patrol guard does not.
- A freed or bought-back player is immune to pinning for 3 s.
- The first backup call still sends 2 officers (existing behaviour); every repeat sends 1. `HeistManager.BackupCalled` is now set on the first call, so the end screen reads "BACKUP CALLED - BUSTED".
- The pinning officer just stands facing the player; there is no kneeling animation yet.
- Pins and the repeat clock are cleared when a new floor starts; busted players stay busted.


### 2.2 Music (`Scripts/Audio/`)

- `MusicDirector` (self-installing, survives scene loads) decides the mood on every machine from state that is already shared; nothing is sent over the network.
  - **Intense**: from the moment backup is called (`HeistManager.BackupCalled/BackupActive`) until the floor is over (elevator seals, run ends, next floor loads).
  - **Elevator** (muzak): while the local player stands in a car, and for 5 s of the ride.
  - Otherwise silence. The docks have no intense music (no guards, no backup).
- **A different intense track on every floor**: `MusicPlan.IndexFor(runSeed, floor)` is a per-run shuffle of the 16 tracks, so nothing repeats within 16 floors, two floors in a row never share a track, and every player hears the same one (the seed is `TowerManager.baseSeed`).
- The tracks are generated by code: `MusicPlan.Tracks` is the "score" (tempo, key, chords, drums, bass, lead per track, edit a line to change one); `ProceduralMusic` renders it on a worker thread when the floor loads, so it is ready long before anybody calls backup. `LoungeMusic` makes the elevator track.
- **Real music**: put clips in `Resources/AudioCatalog.asset` (`intenseMusic`, `elevatorMusic`). They replace the generated ones with no other change (the same shuffle picks the clip per floor).
- Music volume slider in the Esc menu (`MusicDirector.Volume`, saved in PlayerPrefs).
- Verified only by numbers (length, peak, loudness, loop seam, determinism, tracks differ in tone). **Nobody has listened to it.** Expect to tune the mix and the tracks' taste.

### 2.3 Door, elevator and officer sounds

- `SoundId` (`SoundBank.cs`): DoorOpen, DoorClose, ElevatorDing, Keys, KeysRun, RadioStatic, RadioCall. A clip in the AudioCatalog wins (`doorOpen`, `doorClose`, `elevatorDing`, `keys`, `keysRun`, `radioStatic`, `radioCall`); otherwise a placeholder is generated by `ProceduralSound` (a few variants each, made one per frame at startup).
- `GameAudio.PlaySound(SoundId, ...)` plays on a fixed pool of 32 AudioSources (no objects created while playing).
- `GuardSounds` (added to every guard by `GuardVisual`): key jingle every ~7 m walked (about every 3 s walking) / ~6 m running (about every 1.3 s; `walkStride` / `runStride`, each jingle +-25% so it is never a metronome; it was every 2.4 m / 2 m, which was too busy), walkie-talkie static every 7-24 s (first one after 4-18 s), a squelch when he radios for backup. All local to each machine (each guard's rhythm is seeded from his NetId); nothing is networked.

### 2.4 Sliding doors (`Scripts/Doors/`, `FloorGenerator.Doors.cs`)

- A double sliding door in every doorway between rooms (the beam over each doorway marks it). Built **after** the NavMesh bake so guard routes never depend on a door. All doors start shut.
- **Players work them with E, aimed with the crosshair** (section 2.9): point the crosshair at a door (it need not be shut: the doorway counts, and so does the beam over it) while within 2.2 m of the doorway (`DoorManager.UseReach`) and the prompt "[E] Open door" shows; E opens it and it **stays open** until a player presses E again ("[E] Close door"). Walking up to a door no longer opens it, and standing near one without aiming at it does nothing. What E does is decided by what the crosshair is on (item / door / dealer, see `AimMath.Choose`); nothing happens while a menu/shop/panel is open, while hiding or in custody. `DoorManager.Nearest` / `DoorGeometry.UseScore` are the old proximity pick; `UseScore` is still used for the reach test (the host's check too), `Nearest` is no longer called.
- Closing never shuts on something: if a person or a dropped crate is actually **in the doorway** (inside the wall's thickness, `SlidingDoor` clear box = wall depth + 0.03 m) the door stays open (retries every 0.25 s), the prompt says "Something is in the doorway", and it closes by itself once it is clear. Somebody standing beside the door, even right up against it, does not hold it open: E closes it at once. (An earlier version checked 0.35 m each side and made players step away before the door would close.) A player who asks for "close" while standing in the doorway is not squashed; the door closes when he steps out.
- **Officers open doors for any walk through one, and only then** (`GuardAI.GetRoute`, `SlidingDoor.AnyoneWalkingIn`, `DoorGeometry.WalkEnters` / `RouteEnters`). History: be40 made officers open doors only while chasing (the user did not want doors opening for a guard just walking past); be41 added backup officers (they run at the player in the `Investigate` state); be42 (this rule) replaced "which state" by "where is he walking", because the user then reported that officers who go to look in a room walk through the door without opening it. Now an officer in any state that walks about by himself (`GuardAI.UsesDoors`: Patrol, Investigate, Chase; not Calling, Pinning, Stunned, dead) opens a door when **his route enters the doorway's zone within the next 0.9 s of walking** (`SlidingDoor.LookAheadSeconds`; the zone = the gap + 0.25 m each way along the wall, and 0.5 m in front of and behind the wall's middle: `WalkZone`). A guard cannot walk closer than 0.6 m to a wall (agent radius 0.5 + half the 0.2 m wall), so one who walks along a wall or a corridor past a doorway never gets into the zone and opens nothing; one who walks into the doorway does, in time. `DoorManager` collects one `Walker` per officer ten times a second (`GatherWalkers`: his position, then the corners of his NavMesh path, plus his speed) and every door asks whether any of them enters its zone; `GuardNear` is that answer, the door opens while it is true and closes 1.1 s after (never on a body: the physics check of `DoorLogic`). **Host**: the route is the agent's real path (`agent.path.GetCornersNonAlloc`, trimmed to the leg he is on, so a guard who is about to turn into a door is seen early) and the speed is `agent.speed`, so a door opens the moment he sets off; `agent.path` allocates a small object, the doors ask only about guards that are moving, ten times a second. **Remote client** (puppet, no agent): a straight line ahead of the way the puppet faces at its streamed speed (at least 1.5 m/s), so a puppet that turns into a door at the last moment can clip it for a moment. No network traffic: every machine decides for itself. People standing at a door when a floor starts have it snapped open (no sound; players by the old, tighter sensor `SlidingDoor.AnyoneAtStart`, officers only if they stand in the doorway itself `AnyoneInside`); it shuts again when they leave. A throw-away simulation (not in the repo) of an officer setting off from rest or already walking at 1.5-4.6 m/s towards a doorway (straight, or bending at the mouth of the NavMesh opening; narrow and wide door; every scan phase) found the gap at least 1.0 m wide when his body touched the leaves in every case but 20 of 7404 for a guard already running at 4.6 m/s that appears 1.5 m from a shut door, and (puppets) 36 of 8340 sharp bends into the wide door. The generator's speeds are walk 1.5, investigate 2.2, chase 3.6 m/s.
- **Guard-and-door QA scenario** (`NetDevDoors.GuardDoorQa`, part of `NetDevHarness.Doors`; its lines start with `guards:`): finds a doorway with a straight walkable route through it and a route that runs along a wall 1.6-2.4 m from another doorway, blinds one real officer (`sightRange` 0.01, `catchRadius` 0.1) and walks him (a) **through** the doorway four ways: on patrol, going to look at a noise (`NoiseBus.Emit`), sent as backup (`Dispatch`) and chasing (the private `state` / `target` / `unseenFor` fields are set by reflection) - he must get through, the door must be at least 1.2 m open when his body reaches it, and it must be shut again 2.7 s later; (b) **past** the other doorway the same four ways - the door must never open at all; then (c) the **chase length** with the player teleported 8 m in front of him each frame (sight blockers off, range 40): he is still chasing after 7 s, still chasing 3.5 s after the player leaves his cone (range 0.01), a 3-frame glimpse resets the clock, and then he gives up between 4.7 and 5.6 s later (state `Investigate`). If the reflected fields are renamed the scenario logs `SKIP guard QA`.
- Classes: `DoorManager` (one per floor, `DoorManager.Active`) handles the key, the prompt and the officers' scan and moves only the doors in motion; `SlidingDoor` is a plain class, not a component (`Latched` = a player opened it, `GuardNear` = an officer is at it); `DoorLogic` is the pure state machine (`Step`, `CloseNow`; tested outside Unity). The doorway only has a collider while the door is completely shut. At most 3 door sounds per frame.
- **Networked**: a door is addressed by its index in `DoorManager.Doors` (floors are built identically on every machine). The player's machine sends `RunSync.RequestDoor(index, open)` (an explicit wish, not a "flip", so two presses at once cannot cancel); the host checks the player is near the door and not in custody and announces `DoorSetRpc(index, open, epoch)` to everybody including itself; every machine then sets the door. A client that finishes building the floor late asks the host (`RequestDoorStates`) for the list of doors players have opened and snaps them open. Requests/events carry `RunSync.Epoch` so ones for the previous floor are ignored. Offline the door is set directly. The officers' automatic opening stays local on each machine.
- Not done: the player's own machine waits for the host's answer before the door moves (a round trip; no optimistic opening). `DoorManager.UseKey` is fixed to E; it does not follow `Backpack.pickupKey`. `FloorGenerator.slidingDoors` switches all doors off.

### 2.5 Guard speech bubbles

Numbers grow in planner order (west to east, south to north from the elevator corner). A room with two doors has the same number over both.

### 2.7 After hours: ceilings and lights (night look)

The heist is at night in an empty office, so every floor (docks included) has a ceiling, ceiling light panels and a dim night look, and only some areas have their lights on. **Never seen in Unity: expect to tune the numbers below.**

- **Ceiling**: one box tile per 18 m cell (`Ceiling_c_r`, 0.3 m thick, flush with the top of the 3.5 m walls, outer tiles reach over the outer walls), with colliders (the over-the-shoulder camera is sphere-cast against non-rigidbody colliders, so it cannot rise through it). Built **after** the NavMesh bake and after `BuildDoors()` in `FloorGenerator.Generate` (otherwise the ceiling top would be baked as a walkable island); on the docks (no bake) right after `BuildLoadingDock`.
- **Panels** (`Interior/AfterHours.cs`, pure and tested outside Unity): fluorescent 1.2 x 0.6 m panels in a grid per room, corridor, lobby and open zone, 0.3 m clear of the walls, never over an elevator shaft, one inside each elevator car (always lit). Each room is lit or dark by a seeded roll (`AfterHours.LitChance` per theme; the lobby by the elevator and the cars are always lit; every 18 m cell and every ~18 m block is guaranteed some light; about 40 % of all panels are on). The docks and the boss floor are fully lit (`allLit`). Same seed = same lit rooms on every machine, and nothing is networked (purely cosmetic, no `ProtocolVersion` change).
- **Look**: lit panels have an emissive material (warm or cool white, `panelWarmMat/panelCoolMat`, `RealtimeEmissive`); dark panels are grey. One mesh per cell with four submeshes (frame / dark lens / warm lit lens / cool lit lens), freed by `OwnedMesh`; materials are created at runtime and destroyed in `FloorGenerator.OnDestroy` (`DestroyNightMaterials`).
- **Real lights** (`FloorLights`, on the `AfterHours` root): one spot light (125 degrees, pointing down, no shadows, `LightRenderMode.ForcePixel`) per ~4.6 m of lit area, about 100-300 per floor, **all disabled except the ~8 nearest the camera** (within 30 m, rescanned 4 times a second, 0.4 s fade, hysteresis so they do not flicker). Max count = `min(maxActiveLights, 4 + QualitySettings.pixelLightCount * 2)`. Without shadows, light leaks through partition walls onto the floor of the next room; accepted (partition walls have shadow casting off and real-time shadows were too costly). `GuardVision`'s flashlight beam is now `ForcePixel` too, so it works next to the ceiling lights.
- **Night**: `ApplyNightLook` sets flat ambient (`nightAmbient` 0.25/0.27/0.34 on office floors, `dockAmbient` a bit brighter in the basement so the shop is readable), the scene's directional light turned into faint moonlight (`moonIntensity` 0.2, `moonColour`), reflection intensity 0.3 and a darker clone of the skybox (visible only through windows / the sky outside, if any). The scene file itself is not changed.
- **Knobs** (inspector of `FloorGenerator`, header "After hours"): `afterHours` (off = the old open-topped, daylight floors), `nightAmbient`, `dockAmbient`, `moonIntensity`, `moonColour`, `litScale` (0.3-2: share of lit rooms; 1 = ~40 % of panels), `lampBrightness`, `maxActiveLights`. If the dark areas are too dark: raise `nightAmbient` first (the asked-for look is "not too dark overall"). If a lit room looks dim: raise `lampBrightness` (Unity's attenuation is steep, the numbers in `AfterHours.Build` are range 9 / intensity 2.6 for rooms, 10.5 / 2.4 open zones, 7.5 / 2.2 cars). Per-theme lit chances are in `AfterHours.LitChance`.
- **Risks / to check in Unity**: (1) the emission keyword `_EMISSION` on materials made at runtime can be stripped in a player build when no asset uses it (ElevatorPanel and GuardVision rely on the same thing); if lit panels look flat grey in a build, put a material with emission on in a scene or add the Standard shader to Always Included Shaders / use a custom unlit shader; (2) brightness of the ambient and the moon (gamma colour space, `Standard` shader); (3) frame time with 8 forced-pixel lights over 18 m ceiling and floor boxes (lower `maxActiveLights` first, then split the ceiling tiles); (4) Quality levels other than Ultra have `pixelLightCount` of 1-2, so fewer lights are on; (5) the camera clipping into the ceiling when the player stands under a wall (the sphere cast should stop it); (6) NavMesh: nothing from the ceiling should end up in it, since it is built after the bake - open the NavMesh visualisation to confirm.
- Tests: `NetDevTest.LightingTest` (part of `PolicyTest`: deterministic, panels inside rooms and clear of shafts, no overlaps, lobby and cars lit, light in every cell, 30-80 % on, mesh winding) and the "night:" lines of `NetDevHarness.Doors` (ceiling tile per cell with colliders, real lights present, no more than the budget on, dim flat ambient, nothing above the walls but the ceiling).

### 2.8 The elevator interior and its mirror

The service elevator (the 8 x 8 m room in the corner cell; interior 7.6 x 7.6 m) used to be a plain box. It is now dressed like a passenger elevator. **Never seen in Unity** (there is a rough preview made by a test renderer, not by Unity).

- **Look** (planner `Interior/ElevatorLook.cs`, pure, tested outside Unity; mesh by `CarMesh`): wood-veneer wall panels (about 1.25 m wide, dark seams between), cream lower wall, stainless kick plate, chair rail band + handrail all round (the rail stops before the floor-button panel and the GO button), stainless jambs at each doorway (plus a frame on the outside of the front door), a stainless sill, burgundy carpet, a wood ceiling in three strips with nine round downlights (steel ring + glowing lens). The elevator doors themselves are now stainless steel (`CarSteel`). Everything is axis-aligned boxes in car coordinates, trim stands at most 11 cm out of the walls, and **nothing in the interior has a collider** (the camera and players are not affected).
- **Mirror**: the wall opposite the floor-button panel (south wall if the front door is east, west wall if it is north; never a wall with a door) is a mirror, 7.2 x 3.2 m, with a steel frame and the handrail mounted on it. `PlanarMirror` (on the `CarMirror` quad) is the classic planar reflection: when a game camera is about to draw the mirror it renders a second, smaller picture (`quality` x the screen size, default 0.6) with a hidden camera that is reflected in the mirror's plane and clipped at the glass (oblique near plane, `GL.invertCulling`), and the shader `BuildingEscape/ElevatorMirror` (**in `Assets/Resources/Shaders`, so a build always contains it**) shows that picture in screen space. Cost: a second draw of the scene, only while the camera is in front of the glass and either inside the car (`insideBounds`) or has a clear line to it through an open door (rays ignore rigidbodies; wall and shut-door colliders block), and within 16 m. If the shader is missing the mirror falls back to a shiny metal material and a warning is logged.
- **Light**: the planner's ceiling panel in the car is hidden inside the wood ceiling; instead four **point lights** in the car's ceiling (`FloorGenerator.carLampBrightness`, range 8, intensity 2) are added to the `FloorLights` pool (spot lights could not reach the upper walls). The wood ceiling has a slight emission (it gets almost no light from below). Without `afterHours` the car only has the scene's own light.
- **Wiring**: `FloorGenerator.BuildElevatorRoom` calls `BuildElevatorInterior` (only in Play Mode, `elevatorLook`; the editor tools that build floors keep the old box) and builds the doors from the steel material; `ElevatorLook.PanelAlong` is the one place that says where the floor panel hangs (it was a literal 2.9 in two places); `FloorGenerator.OnDestroy` frees the materials and the generated wood texture (`DestroyCarMaterials`); the meshes are freed by `OwnedMesh`; `PlanarMirror.OnDestroy` frees its render texture and camera.
- **Knobs** (inspector of `FloorGenerator`, header "Elevator interior"): `elevatorLook` (off = the old box), `elevatorMirror`, `mirrorQuality` (lower = faster, blurrier), `carLampBrightness`. Colours are in `CarMaterial`; the wood texture is generated by `ProceduralWood` (seed 11, tiles in both directions).

### 2.9 The crosshair: E works what you point at

The player now has a crosshair, and the crosshair decides what E (and Q, and a melee swing) act on. Before, E took "whatever is nearest and in front"; now you point at it. **Never seen or played in Unity** (compiled against stubs; the arithmetic is tested outside Unity).

- **The reticle** (`Interaction/Crosshair.cs`, IMGUI on `Repaint`): a dot with four ticks and a dark shadow at the middle of the screen, scaled with the screen height. White normally; **yellow** when E would work what it is on (an item, a door or the dealer within reach, the ticks close in); **red** when a melee weapon is equipped and a guard or training dummy within 3.2 m is under it; **grey** when it is on something usable that is out of reach. Hidden while the cursor is free or any menu / shop / elevator panel / inventory is open. Knobs: `Crosshair.size`, `Crosshair.show`, and the `PlayerPrefs` key `OvertimeEscape.Crosshair` (0 = hidden; there is no settings-page toggle yet). It installs itself with `[RuntimeInitializeOnLoadMethod(AfterSceneLoad)]` (a `DontDestroyOnLoad` object), so no scene needs changing.
- **The target** (`Crosshair.Aim`, static, worked out once a frame the first time anyone asks, from the camera as last drawn): `AimTarget { Kind (None/Pickup/Door/Dealer), Pickup, Door (index in DoorManager.Doors), Foe, TooFar, Distance }`. The ray goes from the camera through the middle of the screen but **starts at the player's own depth minus 0.25 m** (`AimMath.StartT`), so a wall, desk or the camera collision between the camera and the player never counts, and runs to the player's reach + 1.2 m past his depth. Then (`Crosshair.Resolve`, the testable entry): a thin physics ray finds the first thing in the way (walls, desks, guards; **not** the player himself, teammates or items); items near the player are tested as their collider box **grown 6 cm** (`AimMath.RayItem`, `Pickup.TryGetBounds`) so a mug on a desk is easy to hit, they must be within the backpack's `reach` (2.2 m, measured from the player to the item's nearest collider point) and in front of the first wall (15 cm slack because items rest on surfaces); doors are tested as their doorway box (`SlidingDoor.RayHit`, grown 10 cm sideways, plus 0.7 m above for the beam), within `DoorManager.UseReach`, and a wall in front hides them (25 cm slack for the shut door's own collider); the dealer (`Vendor.Nearby`, only while `CanTalk`) is an upright cylinder of 0.7 m radius around `Vendor.npc` and is **not** hidden by anything (he stands behind a counter). `AimMath.Choose` then picks: the nearest wins, an item beats the dealer beats a door on a tie, and **an item in or in front of the doorway beats the door** (so a crate that keeps a door open can be picked up).
- **Users**: `Backpack` (E picks up the aimed item; if nothing is aimed at it says "Nothing there - aim the crosshair at an item" or "Too far away - get closer"; nothing is said when the crosshair is on a door or the dealer), `DoorManager.HandleUse` (E on the aimed door; the prompt is drawn every frame now, no more 10 Hz scan), `Vendor` (E talks to the dealer only when aimed at him, or to close the open shop; near him without aiming the hint "Aim the crosshair at the dealer to talk" shows), `PlayerGrab.TryGrab` (Q carries the aimed item first, then the old nearest / sphere cast), `MeleeController.Hit` (the hit zone, 1.2 m in front of the player, is centred towards the crosshair: `Crosshair.SwingForward` = the horizontal direction to the point of the aim ray 2.4 m past the player's depth; the facing direction while free-looking or for anyone but the followed player). The old `FindNearest` is only left as Q's fallback; the inventory's "On the ground nearby" list is still proximity (`ScanNear`).
- **Unchanged**: F (sell / elevator panel), H (hide), G, Tab, LMB throw, the networking (the old requests; the host's checks are still the generous ones: 4.5 m for a store, door reach + 1.3 m). Because the target is local, nothing is sent over the network for it and `NetConst.ProtocolVersion` stays "3".
- **Known limits**: with the camera at its steepest (60 degrees down) the crosshair meets the floor about 0.8 m in front of the player, so an item right at his feet or behind him cannot be aimed at: step back or look around (free-look works). The item box is the axis-aligned box of its colliders, so a long item lying at an angle (the crowbar) has a slightly generous corner. The dealer can be targeted through walls inside his talk range. `Pickup`s are transparent to each other only when out of reach.
- **To check in Unity**: (1) the reticle is in the middle, readable on dark and light floors, turns yellow on a nearby mug / crate / door, grey on a door farther than ~2 m; (2) E on an item picks it up only when the crosshair is on it, and not the one next to it; (3) E on a door opens/closes it from the right distance, also while looking at the beam above it, and a door behind a wall cannot be worked; (4) with a crate in an open doorway E picks the crate up, then works the door; (5) the dealer: the crosshair on him turns yellow and E opens the shop, aiming elsewhere in the trailer shows the hint and E does nothing; (6) swing a bat at a guard / the dock's punching bag with the crosshair on it (red) and off to one side; (7) Q on a crate you point at; (8) menus hide the reticle and E in the shop closes it; (9) host + client: same checks on the client (store and door requests are the old ones).
- Tests: `NetDevTest.AimMathTest` (in `PolicyTest`), the "aim:" lines at the end of `NetDevHarness.Doors`, the "dock:" crosshair lines in `NetDevSneak`; the arithmetic was also run with a .NET console test (47 checks).

### 2.10 How a guard sees and how long a chase lasts

Added in be42 at the user's request ("after a guard gets triggered they should continue to chase the person for as long as you are in the cone of vision; if the player leaves the cone for more than 5 seconds the guards stop"). Never played in Unity.

- **The cone** is the fan `GuardVision` draws on the floor: flat, `GuardAI.sightAngle` (65 degrees) each side of the way he faces, `sightRange` (14 m; `hiddenSightRange` 1.5 m for a hiding player) long, cut by anything in the line of sight (`CanSee`'s ray from his eye to the player's chest; the shut door's collider blocks it). `GuardRules.InCone` is the flat test (it used to be the 3D angle from his eye to the chest, which made a player standing right next to him vanish at the edge of the fan); somebody within 0.4 m always counts.
- **The chase** (`GuardAI.UpdateChase`): as long as the target is in the cone, or another free player is (he then switches to that one), the out-of-view clock is zero and he keeps after him, however long that is. Out of the cone **5 s in a row** (`GuardRules.LoseSightSeconds`, `GuardAI.loseSightSeconds`; was 4 s; a glimpse restarts it, `GuardRules.PursuitOver`) and he gives up (`LoseChase`): he walks to where he last saw the player (`Investigate`, at the generator's `guardInvestigateSpeed`, 2.2 m/s), waits 3 s and goes back on patrol; if the player is in his cone again he spots him again (the 2 s "calling backup" pause and the "!" ring come first, as before). During the 5 s he still runs at the player's real position (as before).
- **Not changed**: the first spot is still a 2 s look (`callSeconds`) in which leaving the cone for more than 0.3 s (`loseSightGrace`) drops the call without backup - the "you have 2 seconds to get out of view" rule; a pinned/arrested target ends the chase at once unless another free player is in view.
- Tests: `GuardRules` is pure (`NetDevTest.DoorOpenersTest` runs the checks; the same 47 ran in a .NET console); the chase length is part of `NetDevDoors.GuardDoorQa`.

### 2.11 Guard outposts, the lock hack, notes and floor safes

Added in be43 at the user's request (the red room that is on every floor becomes a guard outpost; a keypad hack to get in; loot that scales with the floor; notes that lead to safes full of cash). **Never played in Unity** - compiled against stubs only (0 errors in the runtime and editor assemblies); the rules and the planner are tested outside Unity (below). Read this section before changing anything in `Scripts/Outpost/`.

**What the player sees**

- On every office floor (not the boss floor) the farthest room of the floor is a **guard outpost** (9.0 x 6.6 m inside, dark slate walls, dark red carpet). Its front wall faces the corridor the cell opens onto: a **long one-way window** (a mirror from the corridor side, an ordinary opening seen from inside) with the **steel door** beside it (a double sliding door like the others, dark steel; each leaf has a tall wired-glass pane over a shorter one, after the reference picture), a red-lit keypad on the wall to the right of the door and a red alarm lamp over it. Inside: three monitoring desks with monitors under the window, a briefing table with a chair and a whiteboard along the east wall, four lockers on the back wall and one on the west wall, wall TVs, a clock, a bin - and in the back-west corner the **small red elevator the backup officers arrive in** (the old red room, kept as an alcove: its door opens into the room, the officers walk out through the room and the front door).
- **Getting in**: aim the crosshair at the keypad (or the door) and press **E**. A panel comes up ("SHORT-CIRCUIT THE LOCK"): after a 0.6 s "hacking" wait a key (**W, A, S or D**) shows in the middle of the screen and you have **1 second** to press it (`HackSession.Window`, plus a 50 ms grace so one slow frame cannot lose it); then the next, **four in all, all different** (each of W, A, S, D once, in random order). Four in time: "ACCESS GRANTED", the lock turns green and the door slides open and stays open (afterwards it is an ordinary E door). A wrong key or a missed second: the lock shorts out - **alarm for 14 s** (`OutpostController.AlarmSeconds`): siren, flashing lamp and keypad, the lock is jammed meanwhile, and **backup is called exactly as when a guard spots a player** (`HeistManager.CallBackupAt`: two officers now, one more every 30 s until the floor ends, the intense music starts). After the alarm stops the hack can be tried again. Esc, walking more than 4.2 m from the keypad, the menu, being grabbed or somebody else's alarm back out **with no alarm**. While the panel is up the player stands still (the keys must not walk him away from the keypad) and the cursor stays locked: the hack is all keyboard (`OutpostUi`).
- **Inside**: the contents are random per run and scale with the floor (`OutpostLoot.Roll(intelSeed, floor)`): a **note** about a floor safe (if this outpost holds one), 0-2 **weapons** (crowbar / bat from floor 1, hatchets from floor 4; chance 40% on floor 1 up to 75% on floor 12) and 0-3 **valuables** (a cash envelope, a gold watch or casino chips, each worth about 3.5% of the floor's quota per unit) lying on the briefing table. Weapons are ordinary `MeleeWeapon` pickups; valuables are `Pickup`s with `bankValue` (carry them to the elevator and bank them like loot).
- **Notes and safes**: about **one floor in five has a safe** (1-12; never more than one per floor; `SafeRules.HasSafe`). The safe is a small steel safe against the wall of one of the numbered rooms. An outpost's note names the room of one safe - on this floor or one or two floors above (the note for a safe sits in the outpost of its own floor 50% of the time, one floor below 30%, two below 20%; an outpost holds at most one note) - and either **gives the 4-digit code** (62% of safes) or says nobody knows it and **a crowbar will open it**. Read a note with E (it stays on the desk); everything you learn goes into your **journal (J)**. The safe's keypad (E) takes the code, with a 1.5 s lock-out after a wrong one; the safe's panel also has a **crowbar** button: 5 s of noisy prying (guards hear it), it **always opens** the safe and the crowbar **breaks 35% of the time** (`SafeRules.CrowbarBreakChance`; the crowbar in hand is used first, else any in the pack). Inside: **cash bundles worth half of the floor's quota** (`SafeRules.Cash`: $2,150 on floor 1 = half of the $4,300 quota; 4 to 8 bundles), real `Pickup`s you carry to the elevator.

**Interpretation decisions (change them if the team disagrees)**

- "The red room" = the red **guest elevator** in the farthest cell (red `guardMat` panels, the backup's arrival car). The outpost takes that cell; the backup elevator lives on as the alcove. The **boss floor** and any floor whose furnishing failed keep the old red car (no outpost there).
- The "security room" of the user's text = the outpost. "Codes (one per floor if there even is one)" = the safes' codes: at most one per floor.
- Notes are **not consumed** and the journal is **per player** (each player reads the notes himself); in a networked run the notes read the same on every machine (they follow from the run seed). If the team wants a shared journal it is `SafeIntel` plus a small RPC.
- Cash is carried and banked like other loot (it counts towards the quota and is paid out as surplus), not added straight to the wallet.
- The hack keys are W A S D (the user's example); the hack is played locally and only its result goes to the host, who checks that the player is near the keypad and not in custody.
- While the alarm sounds the lock is jammed; a lost hack while the alarm is already ringing does not call backup a second time.
- Guards walk through the locked door: it opens for them by the same route look-ahead as every other door (`GuardNear`) without unlocking it, so backup can leave its elevator and a patrolling guard can enter.

Not seen on screen this session, so run the audit once. Also check *Project Settings > Graphics > Always Included Shaders* contains `Standard` for builds.
- Voice: guards hear you by loudness (`VoiceNoise.LevelFromRms`, 3 m at the mic gate to 30 m at a shout). The commit titled "Added pinned state start" contains only this voice work; there was no pinning code in the repo before this session.

## 5. Audit report (multiplayer, reliability, readability, efficiency)

Everything under `Assets/Scripts` and the editor tools was read. Fixed (batch be32):

- **Floor loading**: `TowerManager.LoadFloor` can no longer leave `Loading` set after an exception (the elevator would be dead for the rest of the run); a load asked for while another is running is queued, so two floors are never generated at once. `TowerManager` / `HeistManager` clear their `Instance` on destroy.
- **Multiplayer**: a disconnect re-checks "everybody down"; teammates' puppets no longer stumble twice; Rigidbody interpolation is only on for the body this machine drives (it fought the network transform on puppets); `NetPlayer.IsLocalPlayer` no longer hides the Netcode member of the same name; client puppets that already arrived are not rewritten every frame (loot kept the physics engine busy); the voice receive path reuses its buffer; `ProtocolVersion` bumped.
- **Unity-null safety**: `?.` / `??` on Unity objects replaced with `TryGetComponent` / `if (obj)` where a destroyed object could slip through.
- **Allocation-free UI**: the old HUD made a new native `GUIStyle` for every label on every GUI event (several times a frame). `HudText.Label` now draws on Repaint only with one scratch style; `HudStyles.Label(size, anchor, fontStyle[, colour])` hands out cached styles. About 40 per-event styles were removed. Non-interactive HUDs draw on Repaint only; `GUI.matrix` is restored by the lobby menu and invite popup.
- **Per-frame work**: pickup search is allocation-free (it also had a 64-collider buffer that could miss items in cluttered rooms; now 256); camera-rig lookups are throttled; guard vision cones rebuild every 2nd frame per guard; the crouch scale is only written while it changes; elevator banking returns early when empty; the panel click ray uses a buffer.
- TestMode's level grid had 3 rows (12 buttons) for 14 levels; it now sizes itself.

Checked and fine, no change: custody and backup rules, vendor buy-back, all `Request*` validation on the host, state mirror gating, snapshot format and epoch, NetId assignment, floor checksum, join/leave handling, scene builders, voice.

**Known limitations, deliberately left (decide before a public release):**

- Events (grab, store, consume, spawn) that reach a client **while it is still building the floor** are dropped. Only matters in the first seconds after a floor change; the floor checksum shows the damage. A client-side queue keyed by floor epoch would fix it.
- A player who disconnects loses the loot in their pack (it is destroyed with them).
- Elevator, vendor and hide-spot triggers use `OnTriggerEnter/Exit` per collider; an object with several colliders crossing the edge can flicker in and out for a moment, and a destroyed collider sends no exit.
- Not applied for lack of a profiler: static batching of the floor's boxes, instancing of repeated props.
- `NetPlayer.prefab` and `Greybox.unity` still carry old `VoiceNoise` fields (`threshold`, `maxRadius`); the script ignores them (it uses `minLevel`, `radiusAtGate` 3 m, `radiusAtShout` 30 m) and Unity drops them next time the asset is saved.
- `HideSpot` runs an `Update` per instance and `GuardAI` casts vision rays every frame; fine for the current guard counts, first things to look at if frame rate drops on big floors.

## 6. Known risks / follow-ups

- Network tests (`NetDevHarness`, `NetDevQa`) call `CallBackup`; the 30 s repeat can spawn extra guards in a long test and upset guard-count comparisons.
- Custody physics runs on the machine that owns the body; teleporting a busted body into the elevator resets its rotation and `HoldDown` tips it over again.
- A remote client is not stopped by the host from sending grab/store requests while in custody (only local input is blocked). Fine for friends, not for a public build.
- `HeistManager.Caught`, `maxCaught`, `NotifyPlayerCaught` are legacy and unused.
- After-hours lighting (section 2.7) has never been seen in Unity: the ambient / moon / lamp numbers are first guesses, and without shadows the lamps light through walls. A cheap improvement if that looks bad: turn the lamp's spot angle down, or give only the lamps in the player's room shadows (`LightShadows.Hard`, one at a time).
- The crosshair (section 2.9) has never been seen in Unity: the aim tolerances (item box +6 cm, door box +10 cm and the beam, 25 cm / 15 cm wall slack) and the reticle's size and colours are first guesses. If E feels too fussy on small items raise `AimMath.ItemPad`; if a door is hard to hit raise `DoorPad` / `DoorTopExtra`. No settings-page toggle for the reticle yet (`PlayerPrefs` `OvertimeEscape.Crosshair`).
- The officers' doors (section 2.4) and the 5 s chase (section 2.10) have never run in Unity. Things most likely to need tuning: `SlidingDoor.LookAheadSeconds` (0.9 s) / `WalkZone` (0.5 m) if doors open too early or too late for a guard, and `agent.path` (reads the agent's corridor; if `corners[0]` is not the agent's position the code copes, it looks for the leg he is on). A guard that sets off from rest right next to a shut door can still clip the leaves for a moment (the door needs 0.25-0.35 s). A remote client sees puppets turn into doors with a straight-line guess only.
- The guard outposts (section 2.11) are the largest never-run change: the generator, the planner, the one-way window (a second use of the mirror camera), the networked lock / safe state, the hack and the safe screens. `OutpostStep` keeps a failing step from stopping a floor loading, so look for `[Outpost] Could not build ...` in the console first. The numbers most likely to need tuning: the hack window (`HackSession.Window` 1 s, `Grace` 50 ms, `IntroSeconds` 0.6 s), `OutpostController.AlarmSeconds` (14 s), the odds in `SafeRules` (1 in 5, 62% of notes give the code, 35% crowbar break, cash share 0.5) and `OutpostLoot` (weapon chance 40% to 75%, valuables 3.5% of the quota per unit).
- Nice to have: kneel/pin animation, handcuffed look, pin/bust sounds, tuning of 12 s / 30 s / buy-back price, real music and sound clips.

## 7. Rules of the house

- Scripts are CRLF. If you edit with tools that expect LF, convert, then convert back before saving into the project. New scripts need their `.meta` committed.
- Floor generation must stay deterministic: only `System.Random` for floor content, never `UnityEngine.Random`; creation order matters for `Net.NetIds`. Clients regenerate identical floors from the host's seed. Cosmetic extras (doors, plates, bubbles, sounds) take no NetIds and must not. Content that depends on the run but not on the floor's random numbers (the outposts' loot, the safes, the notes) comes from `SafeRules.Hash(intelSeed, ...)`, never from the floor's `rng`, so it cannot disturb the maze, loot or guards.
- **Edit mode**: `FloorPreviewWindow` calls `Generate` outside Play mode. Code in the generator that needs the running game (components with `Awake`, `Destroy`, audio, the controller, the window, `FillOutpost`) is guarded with `Application.isPlaying`; use the generators' `Kill(...)` instead of `Destroy` for objects made while building.
- The host runs all rules; clients send `Request*` calls (`RunSync.Requests.cs`) and receive events/state (`RunSync.Events.cs`, `RunSync.State.cs`). `Net.NetRole.Simulates` = offline or host.
- **IMGUI**: use `HudStyles` / `HudText.Label` for any new on-screen text, never `new GUIStyle` inside `OnGUI`. Draw non-interactive things on `EventType.Repaint` only. Restore `GUI.matrix` / `GUI.color` if you change them. With GUILayout, draw the same controls on Layout and Repaint.
- **Unity null**: do not use `?.`, `??` or `is { }` on `UnityEngine.Object`s; use `TryGetComponent` or `if (obj)`. Clear a singleton's `Instance` in `OnDestroy`. `OnTriggerExit` is not sent for destroyed objects. Rigidbody interpolation stays off on kinematic puppets moved by the network.
- Pure-logic code (`Scripts/Interior/*` including `FloorShape`, `Scripts/Interaction/AimMath.cs`, `Scripts/Outpost/SafeRules|HackRules|SafeNotes|OutpostLoot`, `CustodyRules`, `MusicPlan`, `ProceduralMusic`, `ProceduralSound`, `DoorLogic`, `SymbolSpeech`) has no Unity types so it compiles and tests outside Unity with plain `csc`; that is the easiest place to add tests.
- Tooling used by the agent sessions (not in the repo): a stub-based compiler check of the runtime and editor code, a .NET console test for the audio/music/door/bubble/room-number logic, and a script that delivers CRLF copies of changed files. Re-create what you need; none of it replaces opening the project in Unity.
